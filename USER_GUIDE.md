# User guide

## What this atlas shows

The atlas shows the geography of survey fieldwork across nine study neighbourhoods in Accra, Johannesburg, and Nairobi. It helps readers understand where the research took place before interpreting the substantive findings.

It does not show individual journeys or the wider geography of residents’ household, social, support, or imagined relations. Those are addressed in the companion **One Life, Many Geographies** interface.

## Basic navigation

1. Select a city from the **City** menu.
2. Select one of its three study neighbourhoods, or retain **All** for the city view.
3. Change the basemap when additional street or visual context is useful.
4. Toggle fieldwork footprints, labels, and the legend as needed.
5. Zoom and pan directly on the map.

Point size indicates the number of interviews represented by an aggregate location. Every point represents at least five interviews.

## Figure presets

The figure selector includes:

- a three-city overview;
- one preset for each city;
- a preset for the current city or neighbourhood selection.

Selecting a preset opens a cleaner figure layout. Use **Back to interactive atlas** to return. Browser Back and the Escape key perform the same action. The selected geography remains available when the normal interface returns.

## Exporting a PNG

1. Choose the desired city, neighbourhood, basemap, labels, and footprint setting.
2. Open figure mode when a clean paper-oriented layout is needed.
3. Allow map tiles and labels to finish loading.
4. Select **Export PNG**.

The figure-mode exit control and other interface-only controls are excluded from the exported image.

The quiet grey basemap is the most reliable option for scholarly figures. Third-party tile restrictions can occasionally prevent a browser from capturing a basemap; changing basemap and retrying usually resolves this.

## Reading the footprints

The shaded hulls summarise the spread of displayed fieldwork locations. They are descriptive envelopes only. They are not:

- official neighbourhood boundaries;
- administrative polygons;
- claims about the full social or spatial extent of a neighbourhood;
- evidence that unsurveyed spaces fall outside the neighbourhood.

## Co-author walkthrough

A concise demonstration can follow this sequence:

1. Begin with the three-city overview and explain the comparative research design.
2. Move through Accra, Johannesburg, and Nairobi to show metropolitan spread.
3. Open one neighbourhood from each city to illustrate local fieldwork footprints.
4. Explain that locations are aggregated and that the hulls are descriptive, not official boundaries.
5. Show a figure preset and use **Back to interactive atlas** to demonstrate the repaired navigation.
6. Conclude by distinguishing this atlas from **One Life, Many Geographies**, which maps aggregated relations extending beyond the study sites.
